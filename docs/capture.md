# Relay

Click the Relay bar at the top of the map (or under Search in the sidebar), or press Command/Ctrl+Shift+K. Settings → Relay changes or turns off that shortcut; it is stored in this browser. Relay is the one verb for handing work off: a typed thought here, or **Relay** on a note (see the README). Thoughts route asynchronously. A thought that matches a project goes to that project's chief as a `<relay>` message. The chief decides what it needs (an answer, a logged fact, a note, canvas cards, images or charts, tasks, a workspace or session for you, a routine, a scout, one worker or several) and settles it with `relay_report`, which makes it a Decision in the Inbox. The thought's **Open session** link is the chief's session. Project tags pin routing. Unmatched thoughts have no chief: they run as a read-only scout in Home, as before.

The router tries twice with a 45-second deadline per attempt. Failed thoughts offer Retry. Thoughts under way are listed at the top of the Inbox. A ready thought appears in Decisions, in time order with the rest, with the worker session and review result. Ready thoughts can continue, receive a redirect, be kept, or discarded. A redirect with another project's tag archives the previous isolated workspace and routes a fresh worker. The original thought remains in the capture text. You can save the redirect as a Home routing rule.

Discard cancels work and archives isolated workspaces; it never archives the shared scout workspace. Undo restores the record and workspace, but does not automatically restart cancelled work. Archived worktrees retain their files under the existing housekeeping policy.

The limit defaults to eight open captures. Set `maxOpen` in `~/.thunk/config/capture.json` (or under `THUNK_DATA_DIR`). Kept and discarded records no longer count. Relay-ready notifications appear in Needs you delivery settings and are the ready thought's row in the Inbox; they close through Keep, Discard, Go ahead or Redirect rather than dismiss.

The desktop app also registers an OS-wide shortcut (default Alt+Space) that opens a small Relay window over whatever app is in front, while Thunk is running. On macOS the window is a non-activating panel: it floats over full-screen apps on any Space, takes typing without bringing the main Thunk window forward, and leaves you in the app you were using when it closes. Pressing the shortcut again closes it. If another app holds the shortcut when Thunk starts, it is off and Settings → Relay says so. Change it there, and turn on Open Thunk at login (macOS and Windows) to keep it working after a restart. It is stored on this computer, including when connected to a remote backend. Submission, Escape and blur hide the capture window. Mobile has a Relay navigation button. Browser drafts survive dismissing the bar within the tab.

First-pass restrictions are worker instructions: no pushing, PRs, merging, deploying or sending before approval. Existing worker tool permission handling still applies. Go ahead authorizes the displayed next step only.

Relay workers receive the original text, the router's provisional brief, and its unverified assumptions. They are instructed to infer the intended outcome, read relevant existing notes, memory and tasks, resolve uncertainty from evidence and preferences, and produce a useful first draft or result. Low-cost reversible assumptions should allow progress; questions are reserved for consequential choices that cannot be resolved from available context. Saved facts need not generate additional work. Scouts can deliver drafts in their response without writing files.

A completed first pass can report `needs_person: none`. It remains available for review, Keep, Discard or Redirect, but has no Go ahead button. Go ahead is available only for a specific held-back step; the server rejects continuation without one. This guidance uses the worker's existing context and tools; it does not add a separate retrieval or review agent, or guarantee that the model follows every instruction.

A chief-handled thought comes back to the Inbox as a conversation with that chief, headed **Chief** with the project's name: what you asked, what the chief reported, and a reply field. A reply goes to the chief as a message and the card returns when it answers, with the whole exchange shown. **Done** closes it, **Go ahead** approves a held-back step, and **Open the chief** goes to its session. These, and Discard, reach the chief as a `<relay_decision>` message. Discard asks the chief to stop the workers it started and cancel their tasks; nothing is archived automatically. If the chief goes idle for five minutes with nothing running or queued and no `relay_report`, the thought is marked failed with Retry; a late report still settles it. A relay counts as your go-ahead, so the chief can start several workers for it without a **Chief plan**. The paragraphs above about worker sessions, diff stats and cost on a ready thought describe Home scouts; a chief-handled thought shows the chief's summary and no cost.
